What you're looking at

Two languages, one event, and a date nobody agrees on.

A chronology entry in Cicero. The Employer's witness statement, in English, puts the site handover on February 19, 2024. Translated from Arabic, the Engineer's letter and the signed handover record both say March 3. Cicero puts all three accounts on the same event and flags the conflict.

Switch the reading language to Arabic and the same entry reads in Arabic, with every original still beside its translation.

How it works

Built for records that cross languages.

Both directions

Translate documents into the language your team works in, and work product into the language the tribunal wants.

Analyzed in the original

Documents are analyzed in the language they were written in, so propositions, events and key players come from the source text, not from a translation of it.

Linked to the source

Every translated passage stays linked to its original paragraph. When it matters what a document actually says, the source is one click away.

One chronology

Events draw on documents in every language in the record. When accounts in different languages disagree, the contradiction is flagged like any other.

Spotlight: Arabic

Arabic and English. One record.

العربية والإنجليزية. ملف واحد.

In the Gulf, the contract is usually in Arabic and the correspondence usually isn't. Cicero translates from Arabic to English and from English to Arabic, analyzes Arabic documents in the original, and puts both languages on the same chronology.

في الخليج، يكون العقد عادةً بالعربية بينما تكون المراسلات بغيرها. يترجم Cicero من العربية إلى الإنجليزية ومن الإنجليزية إلى العربية، ويحلل المستندات العربية بلغتها الأصلية، ويضع اللغتين على تسلسل زمني واحد.
